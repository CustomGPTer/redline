What Redline is

Redline is an interval timer and a training diary. It is not a medical device, a personal trainer or a source of medical, physiotherapy or nutrition advice. The ready-made workouts, the plans, the times, the round counts and the exercise names are general examples. They are not tailored to your health, your fitness or your injuries. Any workout you build or change is your own choice.

Your health

Exercise carries a risk of injury, and in rare cases of serious harm. High-intensity interval training is especially demanding, because it raises your heart rate and breathing close to their maximum.

  • Speak to a doctor before starting this or any exercise programme, particularly if you are pregnant or have recently given birth, are under 18, are over 40 or have been inactive, are overweight, are recovering from surgery, injury or illness, take medication, or have or may have a heart, blood pressure, breathing, back or joint condition.
  • Do not use Redline against medical advice.
  • Stop exercising straight away and seek medical attention if you feel pain, dizziness, faintness, shortness of breath, chest pressure or discomfort, palpitations, nausea or any other symptom that concerns you.
  • Do not train beyond your ability. Warm up and cool down. Make sure you have room around you and a stable, non-slip surface.
  • Hard interval training is not suitable for children.

Technique

Redline does not teach how to perform any exercise. Exercise names are labels for the timer. Doing a movement badly can injure you. Ask a qualified fitness instructor to show you each exercise and correct your form.

Heart rate, zones, calories and statistics

  • Heart-rate readings come from a sensor you choose and pair. Redline does not make or control that sensor, and its readings can be inaccurate, late or missing.
  • Heart-rate zones are calculated from a maximum heart rate that you enter or that is estimated from your age. Such estimates can differ a good deal from your real maximum.
  • Calories, recovery figures, streaks and other statistics are estimates worked out from what you record.
  • None of this is a medical record, a diagnosis or a fitness assessment. Do not use it to detect or manage a medical condition, or to decide whether it is safe to keep going.
  • If you feel unwell, stop, whatever the numbers say. If your doctor has given you a heart-rate limit, follow your doctor.

Timers, sounds and reminders

Android can delay or stop background work, notifications, sounds and speech, particularly on phones with aggressive battery saving. Beeps, spoken cues, vibration, the lock-screen countdown and reminders are a convenience. They are not guaranteed to arrive, or to arrive on time. Do not rely on Redline for anything where timing is critical, or as the official clock for a contest.

Your workouts and what you share

  • Your workouts and your history are yours. The developer never sees them and claims no rights in them.
  • If you choose a sound of your own, use only a sound file that you have the right to use.
  • When you share a workout, the person you send it to receives its name, its description, its times and its exercise names. Share only what you are content for them to see.
  • A workout you receive from someone else was written by them, not by Redline. Look at what Redline shows you before you save it, and treat it with the same care as any workout you have not tried.

Sensors, the scanner, the voice, Health Connect and TVs

Heart-rate sensors are made by other people. QR codes are read by Google's code scanner, spoken cues use the text-to-speech engine installed on your phone, Health Connect is provided by Google, and the TV clock relies on your phone's screen mirroring. These are provided by other people under their own terms. They may be missing on some devices, may behave differently from one phone to the next, and may change. The timer and its beeps work without them.
