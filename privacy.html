Privacy policy

Last updated 17 November 2026. This policy covers the Redline app for Android (package uk.jj.redline) and this website. Redline is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • Your workouts, your history, your heart-rate records and your settings are kept on your phone. They are never uploaded, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • Redline has no accounts and no servers of its own. The developer receives nothing from your phone.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • A workout needs no internet. The app goes online only to ask whether a newer version is out, and the first time you open the QR scanner. The detail is below.
  • Health Connect is off unless you switch it on, and Redline only writes to it. It reads nothing from it.

What stays on your phone

Redline keeps everything in the app's private storage, as small files that other apps cannot read:

  • Your settings. These include your year of birth, your weight and your sex if you enter them. They are used only on the phone, to work out your heart-rate zones and to estimate calories.
  • Your workouts, including any you built, typed or were sent.
  • Your history. For each session that is when it started, which workout it was, how long it lasted, the rounds you finished, estimated calories, and the effort score and notes if you add them.
  • Your heart rate during a session, if you wore a sensor, kept as one file for each session.
  • The name and address of the heart-rate sensor you paired, so that it can reconnect.

If you choose a sound of your own for Go, Redline remembers where that file is. It does not copy it. A workout leaves your phone only when you choose to share it, as a QR code on your screen, a code you copy or a file sent with Share, and what is shared is that one workout: its name, its description, its times and its exercise names. Nothing about you or your history goes with it.

Redline does not ask for your camera, your microphone, your contacts or access to your files. When you pick a sound, a backup or a shared workout, Android's own file picker hands Redline that one file.

Redline does not use your location. On Android 11 and earlier, Android itself only lets an app search for Bluetooth devices if it holds the location permission, so on those phones you are asked for it when you pair a sensor. Redline uses it for that search and nothing else. On Android 12 and later the location permission is not requested at all.

Heart rate and Health Connect

Heart rate is health information, so this is how Redline treats it.

  • Heart rate is read only from a sensor you pair yourself, and only while the app is using it.
  • The readings are kept on your phone with the session they belong to. They are used to draw the graph, to work out your zones, your calories and the beats you recovered, and for nothing else.
  • They are not sent to the developer, not used for advertising and not sold or passed to anyone.
  • Health Connect is off unless you turn it on in Settings. When it is on, Redline writes each finished workout to Health Connect on your phone: the exercise session, the heart-rate readings if you wore a sensor, and estimated calories. From there, apps you have allowed in Health Connect, such as Samsung Health, can read it under their own policies.
  • Redline asks Health Connect only for permission to write. It does not read your steps, your sleep or anything else held there.
  • You can withdraw the permission at any time in Health Connect, and delete there what Redline wrote.

Zones, calories and recovery figures are estimates. They are not a medical reading and Redline is not a medical device.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Sessions stay in History until you delete them. Deleting a session deletes its heart-rate record with it.
  • Workouts you made stay until you delete them. A ready-made workout you delete can be brought back from Settings.
  • A backup file you save is an ordinary file, kept wherever you chose to save it. It holds your workouts, history and settings, and is yours to keep or delete.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling Redline deletes everything it holds on the phone. Save a backup file first if you want to keep it.
  • If your phone's own backup is switched on, Android may include Redline's data in it and bring it back when you set up a new phone. That backup is yours and is managed in your phone's settings.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of Redline holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. Your workouts, history and heart-rate records are on your own phone, where you control them directly using the steps above. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

The app's data is held in private storage that other apps cannot read. The app's connection to GitHub uses HTTPS.

A workout that arrives by QR code, pasted code or file is checked before it is used. Redline reads only the times and names in it, keeps them within sensible limits, and shows you the workout before it is saved.

This website

This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other websites. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub, as the host, receives the internet address of visitors and handles it under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The app file is downloaded from GitHub as well, on the same terms.

Children

Redline is a training timer for adults. Hard interval training is not suitable for children, the app is not directed at them, and its developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
